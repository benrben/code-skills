# Clear text

Use this mode for direct answers, explanations, and procedures that do not need
interaction or motion.

## Apply an STE-inspired style

- Put the answer or main point first.
- Use familiar words, concrete nouns, and active verbs.
- Prefer one main idea per sentence and one topic per paragraph.
- Keep a term consistent instead of rotating through synonyms.
- Name the actor when a pronoun would leave the action unclear.
- Define necessary technical terms where they first matter.
- Put steps in execution order. Separate conditions from actions when useful.
- Keep articles and connective words; short prose should still sound like English.

Aim for short sentences, often around 15–25 words. This is a practical writing
target, not a rule that overrides meaning. Split a sentence when that improves
comprehension; keep it intact when splitting would obscure a condition or cause.

Use connected prose for explanations. Use a list for actual steps or parallel
choices. Retain a natural tone, contractions, and helpful analogies when they
fit the reader. Explain where an analogy stops matching the real mechanism.

## Distinguish clarity from compliance

ASD-STE100 is a controlled English standard with writing rules and a dictionary.
The official rules distinguish procedural and descriptive writing, including
sentence limits of 20 and 25 words respectively. That is only part of the
standard. Short sentences alone do not establish compliance.

For an ordinary explanation, apply the style above. If the user requests strict
ASD-STE100, consult the applicable official issue and dictionary, check technical
terms and meanings, and report the review scope. Do not claim verified compliance
from a prompt or a word-count check. See the official
[Issue 9 standard](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
and [STEMG guidance on AI](https://asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html).

## Example

Dense: “The accumulation of returns attributable to previously realized returns
results in nonlinear growth of the investment balance.”

Clear: “Compound growth means that your returns can also earn returns. If 100
grows by 10%, you have 110. Another 10% increase adds 11, giving you 121.”

Prompt: “Explain compound growth to a beginner. Use ASD-STE100-inspired English,
about 80% of the way. Keep a natural tone and include one numerical example.”

Before sending, check that shortening the prose did not remove a cause,
qualification, uncertainty, or prerequisite.
